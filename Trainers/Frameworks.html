All 8 — what lens are you using?
ItemQuestion it helps answerExam cue
MITRE ATT&CKWhat adversary behaviors/tactics/techniques are being observed?Knowledge base, matrix, tactics/techniques, heat map, TTP mapping.
Cyber Kill ChainWhere is the attacker in the attack progression?Seven sequential stages; disrupt any link.
Diamond ModelHow are adversary, capability, infrastructure, and victim related?Four vertices, pivots, attribution/campaign relationships.
Pyramid of PainWhich indicators are durable and costly for the adversary to change?Hashes at bottom → TTPs at top; higher = more pain.
STRIDEWhat category of threat could affect a system/application?Design/SDLC threat modeling; six threat categories.
CVSSHow technically severe could this vulnerability be?0–10 severity; not exploitation probability.
EPSSHow likely is this vulnerability to be exploited soon?0–1 probability; next ~30 days; complements CVSS.
STIX / TAXIIHow is threat intelligence described and exchanged?STIX = structured intel; TAXII = transport/exchange.
Big discriminator: these are different analytical lenses, not competitors. A single incident may be described with ATT&CK, placed in the Kill Chain, related with the Diamond Model, and prioritized with Pyramid-of-Pain logic.
Cyber Kill Chain — 7 stages
#StageRecognition cue
1ReconnaissanceResearch/scanning/OSINT; identify targets.
2WeaponizationBuild/acquire exploit + malware into a deliverable payload.
3DeliveryTransmit payload: email, website, USB, supply chain.
4ExploitationTrigger vulnerability or human weakness; execute code/gain access.
5InstallationInstall malware/backdoor; establish persistence.
6Command and ControlC2/C&C channel for remote communication/control.
7Actions on ObjectivesExfiltration, lateral movement, ransomware, destruction, mission goal.
R → W → D → E → I → C2 → A
Model limitation: CertMaster notes the Kill Chain is linear and should be used as an analytical guide, not a rigid description of every modern attack.
Diamond Model — 4 vertices
VertexThinkExamples
AdversaryWho?Threat actor/group/operator/customer.
CapabilityHow / with what?Malware, exploit, tool, TTP, tradecraft.
InfrastructureThrough what resources?C2 server, IP, domain, cloud/service account, file-sharing site.
VictimWho/what is targeted?Organization, system, user, data/assets.
Exam clue: Diamond = relationships and pivots, not attack stages. Analysts move between known vertices to generate investigative leads.
Sybex extra: events may also carry meta-features such as time, phase, result, direction, methodology, resources, and confidence. Know this as supporting context, not as extra diamond vertices.
Pyramid of Pain — bottom → top
#LevelMeaning
1Hash valuesTrivial for attacker to change; low adversary pain.
2IP addressesEasy to rotate/change.
3Domain namesSome cost, but still replaceable.
4Network & host artifactsBehavioral/config artifacts become harder to change.
5ToolsForcing tool changes creates substantial attacker cost.
6TTPsHardest to change; highest pain and most durable detections.
Direction matters: move upward = harder for attacker to adapt + more durable detection value.

STIX vs TAXII
  • STIX: standardized, machine-readable way to describe cyber-threat intelligence and relationships.
  • TAXII: application-layer protocol used to transport/share threat intelligence, commonly STIX-formatted data, between systems and organizations.
  • Memory hook: STIX is the language/content; TAXII is the transport/exchange.
Source nuance: do not memorize “XML” as the defining modern STIX clue. Sybex discusses STIX historically as structured markup but its STIX 2.1 example is JSON. The durable distinction is representation/data model vs transport.
