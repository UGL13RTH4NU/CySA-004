Cheat Sheet

Job roles in the SOC (verbatim)
RoleDefinition
Cybersecurity AnalystDetects threats and implements changes to protect the organization.
SOC AnalystResponds to cyber incidents first.
Cybersecurity EngineerBuilds and maintains a system that is safe against cyberattacks.
Incident Response AnalystMinimizes the overall effects of a security breach that has occurred.
Vulnerability AnalystDetects weaknesses and takes measures to correct and strengthen security.
Threat Intelligence AnalystAnalyzes the level of a threat to help inform cybersecurity business decisions.
Threat HunterProactively finds and stops cybersecurity threats before they compromise an organization.
Penetration TesterSimulates real-world cyber attacks to identify vulnerabilities.
Easy trap: Cybersecurity Analyst vs. SOC Analyst. Cyber Analyst = detect + harden. SOC Analyst = first responder to alerts.
Another trap: Threat Intelligence Analyst analyzes threat level to inform business decisions. Threat Hunter proactively looks for threats already inside the environment.
SOC incident lifecycle — 6 steps (verbatim)
#StepWhat happens
1DetectionLogs and network data collected into a central repository (e.g., SIEM). Data analyzed for suspicious activity and anomalous behavior.
2TriagePotential threats are validated, classified, and prioritized.
3InvestigationSOC gathers evidence, builds a timeline, and decides on a response action or escalation.
4ResponseBased on the previous steps, appropriate steps are taken to isolate affected systems.
5Eradication and RecoveryThe threat is removed and vulnerabilities are remediated.
6Post-Incident ImprovementThreat and response are evaluated; processes and systems are updated to prevent similar threats and improve future responses.
Blue team & its tools

Team members responsible for detecting, identifying, and responding to threats = blue team.

  • SIEM — Security Information and Event Management
  • EDR — Endpoint Detection and Response
  • XDR — Extended Detection and Response
  • IDS — Intrusion Detection System
  • NDR — Network Detection and Response
  • Vulnerability scanners

Used to triage alerts, hunt for threats, contain incidents, and communicate risk and remediation to stakeholders.

SOC lifecycle vs. IR lifecycle — watch out

CertMaster Module 1 gives a 6-step SOC workflow. Later modules (and NIST SP 800-61) describe a related but distinct incident response lifecycle — different granularity, different phase names.

Exam guidance: Read the question stem carefully. If it says "SOC workflow" or references Module 1, use the 6-step model (Detection → Triage → Investigation → Response → Eradication and Recovery → Post-Incident Improvement). If it says "incident response lifecycle" and references NIST 800-61, use that framework.
Memory hooks
  • D-T-I-R-E-P — Detect → Triage → Investigate → Respond → Eradicate/Recover → Post-Incident. ("Detectives Type Interesting Reports Every Payday")
  • Triage ≠ Detection. Detection just surfaces potential activity; Triage is where a human validates, classifies, and prioritizes.
  • Investigation informs Response. You don't isolate systems until Investigation has built the picture.
  • Response ≠ Eradication. Response = contain / isolate. Eradication = remove threat + patch vuln.
  • Post-Incident Improvement is the "lessons learned" phase — updates playbooks and controls.
SOC-analyst perspective
  • Detection fires an alert → you (SOC Analyst) triage it. Most alerts are noise; your job is to decide which few are worth escalating.
  • Escalation from Investigation typically hands off to the Incident Response Analyst for containment/response, and to Vulnerability Analyst for remediation once eradicated.
  • Threat Hunter works largely outside the alert-driven lifecycle — they build hypotheses and hunt in the same telemetry the SOC uses reactively.
